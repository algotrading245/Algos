### 1. Speculative pressure in commodity, currency and equity-index futures (monthly)

- **Source:** J. H. Fan, A. Fernandez-Perez, A.-M. Fuertes, J. Miffre, "Speculative Pressure", Journal of Futures
  Markets 40(4), 2020, pp. 575-597, DOI 10.1002/fut.22085. Accepted version (open access, City Research Online):
  https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23283/ (PDF
  https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23283/1/FINAL_JFM__Accepted_27Nov2019_SSRN.pdf, 40 pages; saved to
  `marketdata/papers/`).
- **Rule (as far as read):** each month, rank the futures in each class by a speculative-pressure signal built from
  the net positions of speculators (or hedgers) in the weekly Commitments of Traders data. Go long the top-ranked
  contracts and short the bottom-ranked, as a monthly long-short portfolio. The paper reports premia in commodity,
  currency and equity-index futures, none in fixed income. The exact signal and the weights are in sections I did not
  read.
- **Claims (page 12 of the accepted version):** annualised Sharpe 0.61 (commodity), 0.47 (currency), 0.50 (equity
  index); CER 2.97%, 1.74%, 2.39% a year. Backwardated contracts earn 2.47% to 5.29% a year, contangoed ones lose
  -2.42% to -5.77%. The text says the premium "is not driven by transaction costs or illiquidity" and is robust to
  alternative signals and sub-periods; I found no cost figure in basis points in the passages read. Fixed income
  gives Sharpe -0.28.
- **Instruments:** the FTMO set has XAUUSD, the FX pairs (USDJPY, EURUSD, AUDCAD) and US500.cash / US100.cash. The
  paper's cross-section is wider (about 20+ futures per class), so a 4-6 instrument version is a different portfolio,
  not a replication, and the test has to say so.
- **Holding time:** about one month (monthly rebalance; the signal is known a few days after its weekly as-of date).
- **Data needed:** CFTC Commitments of Traders history for gold (COMEX), the JPY, EUR, AUD and CAD futures, and the
  equity index futures; prices as continuous futures or FX spot. None is in the repo, and the researcher had no
  download route to the CFTC in this run; a downloader would have to go into `tools/` (agents do not edit tools/).
- **Why it might be uncorrelated with the Hydra legs:** it trades once a month on a slow positioning signal, so it does
  not share the legs' intraday windows (breakout 10:00-18:00 server, noise leg 10:00-16:00 New York, fix, bell,
  tokyo). Its P&L is driven by positioning, not by intraday moves. Check correlation with the noise and breakout legs
  and with the trend legs the book already has.
- **Why it might not survive the gates:** (1) the Sharpe 0.47-0.61 comes from a wide multi-market cross-section; on
  4-6 instruments it is likely much lower, so the DSR bar (0.9 at N trials) is hard to clear. (2) Monthly returns
  over 2000-2017 give about 200 observations; at Sharpe 0.5 for about 17 years t is about 2.1, short of a
  multiple-testing bar. (3) The rejected "Daily and slow strategies" and "Re-weighting the book" families say slow
  strategies sit at Sharpe 0.5-0.7 at best; the gain to a book at Sharpe 3.4 is small. (4) Monthly rebalancing costs
  are not measured here. (5) The companion paper, Kang, Rouwenhorst and Tang, "A Tale of Two Premiums" (JF 2020,
  DOI 10.1111/jofi.12845; title and abstract only in this run), is the same commodity idea split into commercial and
  non-commercial positions, so it is a variant of this one and is not proposed separately.
- **Prior:** low. Listed because it is a flow-based reason (hedger and speculator position transfer), not a time slot,
  and because the project has not tested this family.
