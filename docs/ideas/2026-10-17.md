## 1. Treasury auction, then macro release, then FX (Krohn and Vala 2025)
- Source: I. Krohn and R. Vala, "Auctions, Announcements, and Abnormal Returns", SSRN 5207418 (2025). Read only as a
  newsletter digest.
- Rule (as digested): when a macro release (GDP, CPI, payrolls) comes right after a US Treasury auction, non-USD G9
  currencies gain about 8 bp against the dollar. Other announcement days show little. The digest gives no entry time,
  hold or direction rule.
- Instruments: EURUSD and USDJPY. Holding time is minutes to hours.
- Data needed: Treasury auction and macro release calendars (not in the repo), plus histdata M1.
- Risks: there are few events, so the yearly Sharpe is likely low. The timing (08:30 New York) sits inside the breakout
  leg's window.

## 2. Round-number stop cascades in USD/JPY and EUR/USD (Osler, NY Fed SR125 and SR150)
- Sources: C. L. Osler, "Currency Orders and Exchange-Rate Dynamics: Explaining the Success of Technical Analysis"
  (https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf), and "Stop-Loss Orders and Price
  Cascades in Currency Markets" (SR150, 2002).
- Rule (as summarised): stop and take-profit orders cluster at round numbers. Take-profits stop trends there, and
  stop-losses speed them up once the level is crossed. To test: on a fast cross of a whole figure in USDJPY or EURUSD,
  trade in the cross's direction for a fixed time or until a stop.
- Data: histdata M1, already in the repo.
- Risks: the move has to clear about 1.2 bp of cost. It is close to the rejected channel breakout and overlaps the
  USD/JPY breakout leg's window, so its correlation with that leg must be checked.

## 3. S&P 500 futures pinning on serial expiry days (Golez and Jackwerth 2012)
- Source: JFE 106(3):566-585. The working paper is
  http://www.uni-konstanz.de/FuF/wiwi/workingpaperseries/WP_Jackwerth-Golez-12-10.pdf
- Hypothesis (ours): on expiry days, US500 drifts toward the nearest round strike (25 or 50 points) in the afternoon.
  The paper's strike comes from open interest, which we do not have.
- Claims: the abstract gives no Sharpe or cost figure. An unverified secondary source says pinning has faded since 2016.

## 4. Expiry-week drift before monthly index expiry (Tosi and Ziegler)
- Source: secondary summary only (cxoadvisory). The paper's trade is short puts, which FTMO does not offer.
- Proxy to test: long US500/US100 from about five trading days before monthly expiry until expiry day.
- Overlap: this is a calendar effect close to the rejected turn-of-month family. It has to show it differs from that
  family.
