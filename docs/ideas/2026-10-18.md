## 1. Volatility window around Chinese macro releases, then a break in AUD/USD (data release)

- **Source:** T. Mathews, "The Effect of Chinese Macroeconomic News on Australian Financial Markets", RBA Bulletin,
  December 2016. https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2016/dec/6.html (HTML; the PDF
  `.../rba-bulletin-2016-12-the-effect-of-chinese-macroeconomic-news-on-australian-financial-markets.pdf` returned 403).
  Sample January 2000 to November 2016, intraday data from Thomson Reuters.
- **What it claims (Table 2 and Table A1 as the page gives them, AUD/USD "additional volatility", per cent, the
  coefficient on the absolute change in a window running 10 minutes before to 10 minutes after each release):**
  China GDP 0.01 (2000-07), 0.06*** (2008-16), 0.05*** (full); China activity -0.01, 0.04***, 0.02**; China trade
  0.01*, 0.03***, 0.02***; China CPI 0.03***, 0.04***, 0.01 (full sample not significant). For comparison US labour
  force 0.16, 0.20, 0.18 and US GDP 0.09, 0.05, 0.07. The article says China activity volatility "has roughly doubled"
  in the recent period. **The paper measures size, not direction.** Its regression takes absolute changes.
- **Rule (ours, to test):** on each scheduled China release (GDP, activity, trade, CPI; release times to be taken from
  the NBS calendar), take the high and low of the 10 minutes before the release as the range. From the release until
  60 minutes later, buy AUD/USD on a break above the range or sell on a break below it, once per side per release
  (the same one-shot logic as the USD/JPY breakout leg). Stop at the other side of the range. Flat after 60 minutes
  or at 06:00 server, whichever is first. Instruments: AUDUSD first (histdata M1 in the repo); AUDCAD as a second
  check. Holding time 15-60 minutes.
- **Data needed:** histdata AUDUSD M1 (in repo), the release timestamps for China GDP, activity, trade and CPI
  2010-2026 (not in the repo; the NBS calendar), spread at 0.8-1.0 bp assumed on AUDUSD (not measured here; the
  breakout's 1.2 bp was measured on USDJPY ticks).
- **Event count:** roughly 40 releases a year (GDP quarterly, activity, trade and CPI monthly), so about 600 since 2010.
  That is enough for a Sharpe test.
- **Why it might be uncorrelated:** the releases come in the Asian night, roughly 01:00-04:00 UTC. The USD/JPY breakout
  trades 10:00-18:00 server, so the windows do not overlap. The AUDCAD grid in Hydra trades AUD/CAD, so check its
  correlation with the AUD leg (both are AUD).
- **Why it might not survive costs or the gates:** the paper gives extra volatility of 4-6 bp of absolute move in
  the 20-minute window, against about 1 bp round trip, but it does not say which way AUD moves. A breakout gets the
  direction from the first break, and that break may be noise in both directions: the same 20-minute move can run
  against the first break. The 2008-16 effect is the main one; the full-sample CPI result is not significant. A
  two-sided break is close to the rejected channel-breakout family, so the difference must be shown (event-timed
  windows, not a channel). The US release rows of the same table overlap the rejected announcement-drift family
  (payrolls and FOMC drift); the US releases are not part of this idea.
- **Prior:** low to medium. The volatility effect is real in the paper's own table, but a direction is not.

## 2. S&P 500 quarterly rebalance close: index-level flow on the rebalance Friday (market structure)

- **Sources:** (a) NYSE Research, C. Li, "Closing Auction: Liquidity momentum around rebalances" (August 2022),
  https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/closing-auction-liquidity-momentum-around-rebalances (a data note, not a paper;
  the page did not fully load, so only its summary is known here). It reports that closing auctions for S&P 1500 or
  Russell 1000 names grow about two days before the quarterly S&P rebalance and stay elevated for about two days
  after, in 2021-22. (b) H. Chen, G. Noronha and V. Singal, "The Price Response to S&P 500 Index Additions and
  Deletions: Evidence of Asymmetry and a New Explanation", Journal of Finance 59(4), 2004, pp. 1901-1929, DOI
  10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00683.x. Abstract only: added firms keep a permanent price rise, deleted firms show no
  matching fall; they attribute it to investor awareness. (c) Kumar, Lawrence, Prakash and Rodriguez, Journal of
  Banking and Finance (2023): dispute the asymmetry and find a permanent response for both (abstract only, via a
  search snippet).
- **Rule (ours, to test):** on the third Friday of March, June, September and December (the quarterly rebalance
  effective date), take the US500.cash move from 15:00 to the 16:00 New York close as the flow window, and hold to
  the next day's 09:30 cash open. Test both directions, and also whether the index's moves on those days show the
  auction-volume pattern at all. This is a flow test, not a sign prediction: the paper evidence is at stock level, and
  the index-level direction is not given by any source found.
- **Instruments and data:** US500.cash (histdata SPXUSD M1 is in the repo; check that it is the cash index, not the
  futures); also US100.cash, which the Nasdaq rebalances affect.
- **Holding:** from 15:00 to the next 09:30 New York (about 18 hours).
- **Event count:** four a year, so roughly 64 events 2010-2026. That is far too few for a Sharpe test to clear the
  gates (DSR needs a long sample). The test is worth running only as a check on whether the flow shows up in the
  index at all.
- **Why it might be uncorrelated:** it is one holding per quarter, almost no overlap with the legs. Check it against the
  noise-area leg, which closes at 16:00 New York on the same days.
- **Why it might not survive costs or the gates:** the index is roughly net-neutral across index funds, so the flow
  may not reach the index level; the stock-level effect is about single names, which we cannot trade. The sample is
  too small to show a Sharpe. Its overlap with the rejected expiry-pinning and expiry-week family (both abstract-only
  ideas in the 2026-10-17 file) is high: quarterly rebalances fall on quarterly expiry Fridays. Expect the orchestrator
  to reject it unless the 15:00-close move already shows at index level.
- **Prior:** very low. Listed because the flow is a reason, not a time slot.
