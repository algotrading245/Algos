### 1. Speculative pressure in commodity, currency and equity-index futures (monthly)

- **Source:** J. H. Fan, A. Fernandez-Perez, A.-M. Fuertes, J. Miffre, "Speculative Pressure", Journal of Futures
  Markets 40(4), 2020, pp. 575-597, DOI 10.1002/fut.22085. Accepted version (open access, City Research Online):
  https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23283/ (PDF
  https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23283/1/FINAL_JFM__Accepted_27Nov2019_SSRN.pdf, 40 pages; saved to
  `marketdata/papers/`).
- **Rule (as far as read):** each month, rank the futures in each class by a speculative-pressure signal built from
  the net positions of speculators (or hedgers) in the weekly Commitments of Traders data. Go long the top-ranked
  contracts and short the bottom-ranked, as a monthly long-short portfolio. The paper reports premia in commodity,
  currency and equity-index futures, none in fixed income. The exact signal and the weights are in sections I did not
  read.
- **Claims (page 12 of the accepted version):** annualised Sharpe 0.61 (commodity), 0.47 (currency), 0.50 (equity
  index); CER 2.97%, 1.74%, 2.39% a year. Backwardated contracts earn 2.47% to 5.29% a year, contangoed ones lose
  -2.42% to -5.77%. The text says the premium "is not driven by transaction costs or illiquidity" and is robust to
  alternative signals and sub-periods; I found no cost figure in basis points in the passages read. Fixed income
  gives Sharpe -0.28.
- **Instruments:** the FTMO set has XAUUSD, the FX pairs (USDJPY, EURUSD, AUDCAD) and US500.cash / US100.cash. The
  paper's cross-section is wider (about 20+ futures per class), so a 4-6 instrument version is a different portfolio,
  not a replication, and the test has to say so.
- **Holding time:** about one month (monthly rebalance; the signal is known a few days after its weekly as-of date).
- **Data needed:** CFTC Commitments of Traders history for gold (COMEX), the JPY, EUR, AUD and CAD futures, and the
  equity index futures; prices as continuous futures or FX spot. None is in the repo, and the researcher had no
  download route to the CFTC in this run; a downloader would have to go into `tools/` (agents do not edit tools/).
- **Why it might be uncorrelated with the Hydra legs:** it trades once a month on a slow positioning signal, so it does
  not share the legs' intraday windows (breakout 10:00-18:00 server, noise leg 10:00-16:00 New York, fix, bell,
  tokyo). Its P&L is driven by positioning, not by intraday moves. Check correlation with the noise and breakout legs
  and with the trend legs the book already has.
- **Why it might not survive the gates:** (1) the Sharpe 0.47-0.61 comes from a wide multi-market cross-section; on
  4-6 instruments it is likely much lower, so the DSR bar (0.9 at N trials) is hard to clear. (2) Monthly returns
  over 2000-2017 give about 200 observations; at Sharpe 0.5 for about 17 years t is about 2.1, short of a
  multiple-testing bar. (3) The rejected "Daily and slow strategies" and "Re-weighting the book" families say slow
  strategies sit at Sharpe 0.5-0.7 at best; the gain to a book at Sharpe 3.4 is small. (4) Monthly rebalancing costs
  are not measured here. (5) The companion paper, Kang, Rouwenhorst and Tang, "A Tale of Two Premiums" (JF 2020,
  DOI 10.1111/jofi.12845; title and abstract only in this run), is the same commodity idea split into commercial and
  non-commercial positions, so it is a variant of this one and is not proposed separately.
- **Prior:** low. Listed because it is a flow-based reason (hedger and speculator position transfer), not a time slot,
  and because the project has not tested this family.

### 1. Japanese yen intervention days, USD/JPY continuation (unverified, low prior)

- **Source:** C.-N. Chen, T. Watanabe, T. Yabu, "A New Method for Identifying the Effects of Foreign Exchange
  Interventions", Hitotsubashi IFD Working Paper 73, first draft Oct 2005, this version May 2011:
  https://www.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/ifd/doc/IFD_WP73.pdf (34 pages; saved to `marketdata/papers/`). Practitioner note:
  StoneX, "Japanese yen intervention: volatility fades but turning points emerge":
  https://www.stonex.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/japanese-yen-intervention-volatility-fades-but-turning-points-emerge/
- **Rule (proposed, not in the paper):** on a day Japan's Ministry of Finance is reported to have intervened to buy
  yen, short USD/JPY from the first M1 bar after the report, with a stop at the day's pre-report high, and flat two
  hours later. A second version takes the opposite side (fade) so the test can show which way the intraday move goes.
  The paper gives the effect size only: one trillion yen moves USD/JPY by about 1.7% (Bayesian data augmentation,
  intraday amounts reconstructed), more than twice the OLS estimate from daily data. It does not give a post-event
  path, a return by hour or a trading result.
- **Instruments:** USDJPY (FTMO). The yen crosses (EURJPY, AUDJPY) are the same event on a second pair, not a
  separate idea.
- **Holding time:** up to two hours, intraday, inside the London and New York sessions (the rejected USD/JPY breakout
  family sits in the same hours; the test must show overlap with the breakout leg, `breakout` in
  `docs/candidates.json`).
- **Claims and sample:** the paper's sample is the 2001-2004 disclosure period (quarterly disclosure of daily totals
  at the time). The practitioner note reports USD/JPY down about 1.2% on intervention days, median -1.45%, with
  ranges widening on the day and contracting after; the note says the sample is small and treats the figure as
  anecdotal. I did not check the count of days in any source, so the sample size is not verified.
- **Data needed:** the exact time of each intervention. The MOF publishes only daily totals, with a lag. Without
  intraday timestamps the trigger cannot be rebuilt from repo data (histdata M1 has the price, not the action). A
  real-time trigger would need a dated newswire archive of intervention reports, which the repo does not have.
- **Why it might not survive costs and the gates:** (1) the effect is an immediate move, so the first M1 bar after the
  report may already carry most of it; the continuation after that is the unknown the paper does not measure.
  (2) Few days: a 2001-2026 record of intervention days is a few dozen, far short of what the DSR bar (0.9) needs for
  a test on the sample we have. (3) The report time is public only after the fact in many cases, so a real-time
  version is harder than the paper's ex post version. (4) Intervention is the central bank's own reaction to the
  move, so the trigger may be a symptom of a trend the trend legs already see. Prior: **low**. Listed because it is a
  flow with a reason (official buying of yen), not a time slot. The orchestrator should drop it if the timestamp data
  cannot be found.

### 2026-10-19 run 6 / 1: FX carry with crash risk (Brunnermeier, Nagel, Pedersen)

- **Source type:** paper (NBER working paper; published in NBER Macroeconomics Annual 2008).
- **Title and authors:** "Carry Trades and Currency Crashes", Markus K. Brunnermeier, Stefan Nagel, Lasse H.
  Pedersen, 2008.
- **Link:** [NBER w14473 PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf) (read via `tools/pdf_text.py`; the PDF is 36
  pages and is cached in `marketdata/papers/www.nber.org_papers_w14473.pdf`).
- **Rule:** each week (or quarter) go long the k currencies with the highest 3-month interbank rate and short the k
  with the lowest, equal weights, against the USD. The paper's data are daily spot and 3-month rates for AUD, CAD,
  JPY, NZD, NOK, CHF, GBP and EUR, 1986-2006. Its point is that the carry return has negative skew (crashes when
  carry unwinds in risk-off), tied to funding liquidity and the VIX.
- **Instruments on FTMO:** AUDCAD, USDJPY and EURUSD, plus the other majors if the broker offers them. Only three
  pairs are available for the k = 1 book, so the diversification the paper's k = 2 and 3 books rely on is missing.
- **Holding time:** weekly or quarterly rebalance; positions held for days to months. The slow family.
- **Paper's reported figures (Table 2, gross, no costs given):** annualised Sharpe 0.70 (weekly) and 0.65 (quarterly)
  for 1 long / 1 short; 0.59 and 0.64 for 2 / 2; 0.75 and 0.78 for 3 / 3, over 1986-2006. Skewness -0.5 to -1.0 in
  the weekly and quarterly series. The paper says skew does not diversify away across currencies.
- **Costs:** not reported in the paper. Spreads for majors are small, but the book turns over only weekly, so cost
  is probably not the binding constraint; the crash risk is.
- **Why it might not survive:** (1) it is a slow daily-to-monthly book, the family the rejected list drops for
  currency momentum and monthly FX (ideas 2026-10-17), so the orchestrator must name what differs; the signal
  differs (interest differential, not past return), but the book has the same size problem (3 pairs, weekly). (2) The
  crash risk is the carry's own and it arrives in risk-off, when the US500 and US100 legs also fall, so it may add
  risk rather than hedge it. (3) The sample ends in 2006: the Sharpe is pre-2008 and pre-zero-rate, and the
  post-2008 carry premium is weaker (the same fading that Table 10 of the speculative-pressure paper showed for
  currency SP). (4) Not tested against the Hydra legs, which are intraday; a daily carry book would have to
  hold through the day, and the legs' margin would then be shared.
- **Data needed:** daily spot (histdata has minute spot for the pairs, so daily closes can be built) and daily or
  weekly 3-month rates for each currency (not in the repo; no fetch done). Interest-rate data would need a new
  source, for example the central banks' or FRED series, which the researcher has not checked.
- **What it claims and how it would be tested:** the claim is a positive carry Sharpe with crash risk. A test would
  have to show net Sharpe >= 0.5 in both halves (2010 on, 2018 on, with 2010-2017 and 2018-2025.09) at real costs,
  and the crash drawdown of the book would have to fit the funded-account limits (the FTMO 3% daily loss and the 10%
  total loss).

### 1. End-of-day rebalancing flow of leveraged ETFs, US index last hour (unverified, low prior)

- **Source:** Rodier, Haryanto, Shum, Hejazi, "Intraday Share Price Volatility and Leveraged ETF Rebalancing" (2012),
  as described in a CXO Advisory post: aggregate leveraged-ETF rebalancing is said to explain about a third of
  late-day stock-return volatility, and the post reports a strategy on anticipated rebalancing pressure. Companion
  lead: Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi, Moerke, SFI WP (2022), which links leveraged-ETF rebalancing and options
  delta-hedging to end-of-day return momentum and mean reversion (stock prices, not index futures).
  Links: https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/front-running-leveraged-etfs-at-the-end-of-the-day ;
  https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-22-40-liquidity-provision-to-leveraged-etfs-and-equity-options-rebalancing-flows-evidence-from-end-of-day-stock-prices
- **Rule (proposed, not in either source as a tested rule):** on a US-index session where the S&P 500 or Nasdaq 100
  has moved by more than a set size from the prior close, estimate the leveraged-ETF reset as the day's move times the
  leveraged AUM (2x and 3x funds rebalance in the direction of the day's move). Trade US500.cash or US100.cash in
  the direction of the day's move from about 15:00 New York to the close, with the stop on the day's extreme.
- **Instruments:** US500.cash, US100.cash (FTMO). The leveraged AUM series is not in the repo.
- **Holding time:** under one hour, intraday, inside the New York session.
- **Claims:** the rebalancing share of late-day volatility (about a third, per the CXO summary). No Sharpe, no hit
  rate and no cost figure were seen, so the claim is not a trading result.
- **Data needed:** minute US500/US100 (histdata, present in the repo) for the trade; daily leveraged-ETF AUM
  (not in the repo, needs a new source and a downloader in `tools/`).
- **Why it might be uncorrelated with the Hydra legs:** the reason is a flow (forced rebalancing), not a time slot,
  and the noise leg already trades the US indices in New York hours, so the overlap must be measured.
- **Why it probably does not survive the gates, and the overlap with a rejected family:** the orchestrator must
  judge this. The rule is a same-day own-move continuation in the last hour, which the rejected list already covers as
  "market intraday momentum (unconditional, both-agree, big-move)", "shorter last windows" and "next-day fade of the
  last half hour". The only named difference is the AUM-scaled trigger; without an AUM series it reduces to the
  rejected big-move rule. Prior: **low**. Listed because it is a flow reason and the AUM trigger is not in the
  rejected tests. The orchestrator should drop it if the AUM data cannot be found.
